Costa Rica.

Two nights in San José for art and theater, two beside the Caribbean canals for sea turtles, two at an active volcano for wildlife and hot springs, three on the Pacific coast for sloths, monkeys, and snorkeling. No car needed. The whole flight is nine hours.

Day by day

Ten nights in Costa Rica. Move mornings; afternoons rain.

Day 1
Sunday, August 9 · bienvenidos a San José

Land early afternoon — the SJO connection through Houston is ~8.5 hours total, one of the shortest international runs from San Diego. Settle into a boutique hotel in Barrio Amílcar Arias, the neighborhood that's become San José's arts district: independent galleries, colorful murals, small restaurants, the kind of place you walk around without an agenda. A coffee, an easy dinner.

Day 2
Monday, August 10 · the theater and the art

The morning at Teatro Nacional — Costa Rica's 1897 neo-baroque opera house, built by coffee barons who taxed their own exports to fund it. Marble staircases, a gilded chandelier, painted ceilings. English-language tours run daily; it's compact enough that you feel every corner. After lunch, the Museo de Arte y Diseño Contemporáneo (MADC) for Central American contemporary work that doesn't travel north. Then an afternoon through Barrio Escalante — the murals change seasonally, and there's always something going on in the small galleries off the main street.

Day 3
Tuesday, August 11 · into the canals

Transfer day. A shuttle east to La Pavona dock, then 1.5 hours by flat-bottomed boat through jungle waterways into Tortuguero — a village accessible only by boat or small plane; no roads in or out. Arrive early afternoon. A first slow canal tour from the lodge: river otters, caimans, Jesus Christ lizards running across the water, all three monkey species working the riverbank.

Day 4
Wednesday, August 12 · turtles

An early morning kayak on the canals — the light is green and still before 7am, and the animals are out. A walk through Tortuguero village itself: painted wooden houses, an Afro-Caribbean culture distinct from the Spanish-influenced mainland, a handful of artisan spots. August and September are peak nesting months for green sea turtles — the most important nesting site in the Western Hemisphere. The night beach tour departs at 8pm or 10pm (groups capped at 15, guides certified by Costa Rica's Ministry of Environment): you may watch a 300-pound turtle come ashore, dig, and lay. Book through your lodge on arrival day; slots fill fast.

Day 5
Thursday, August 13 · the volcano

Boat back to La Pavona, then shuttle northwest toward Arenal. The approach to La Fortuna is one of the more dramatic in Central America: the volcano emerges from cloud as you get close, a near-perfect cone 5,437 feet above flat cattle country. Check in, short walk around the lodge grounds. Clear mornings are the window — afternoons in Arenal are reliably rainy. Tonight: hot springs.

Day 6
Friday, August 14 · bridges, birds, springs

A morning on the hanging bridges that run through the Arenal rainforest canopy — 16 bridges total, the longest a 100-meter suspension crossing above the treetops. Toucans, three-toed sloths, emerald toucanets, howler monkeys. The afternoon is for rain — Arenal gets heavy daily afternoon showers in August, which is exactly why the evening hot springs work so well: volcanic-heated pools, built into the jungle, available well into the night.

Day 7
Saturday, August 15 · artisans and the Pacific

Transfer south toward Manuel Antonio. En route: a stop in Sarchí, the artisan town where families have been painting the geometric oxcart patterns — declared UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage — for generations. Watch a carver work; the patterns are intricate, specific to each workshop, and take hours per panel. Then the road drops toward the Pacific coast and the temperature changes. Arrive Manuel Antonio late afternoon.

Day 8
Sunday, August 16 · the park

An early entrance to Manuel Antonio National Park — arrive by 7am before the heat and crowds. This is the most reliably wildlife-dense park in Costa Rica: white-faced capuchin monkeys walk the trail at knee-height, entirely unbothered. Three-toed sloths hang in the cecropia trees near the entrance. Scarlet macaws in the canopy. Raccoons on the beach. The park closes at 4pm; spend the morning inside and afternoon at the lodge pool. Mornings here are clear; rain comes by 2pm.

Day 9
Monday, August 17 · beach and snorkeling

A slow morning — coffee, no plan. Then to Playa Biesanz, the small protected cove near the park entrance: calm clear water, reef fish, rays, occasional sea turtles. Snorkel gear is available for rent nearby. This is beginner-friendly snorkeling — a protected bay, no current, good visibility in the morning before afternoon rain stirs up the surface. Lunch at one of the beachside places, afternoon back at the lodge.

Day 10
Tuesday, August 18 · the road back

A slow morning. Shuttle north to San José — about 3.5 hours. The city is worth one more evening: a final dinner in Barrio Escalante, the neighborhood that started the trip. Early night; SJO departs early.

Day 11
Wednesday, August 19 · the short flight home

SJO is 15 minutes from the hotel district. Connect through Houston or Dallas; home by mid-afternoon. Total return travel: 9–10 hours — a normal travel day.

Lodging

San José — Barrio Amílcar Arias / Barrio Escalante

  • Boutique hotel in the arts district, walking distance to Teatro Nacional and MADC
  • Look for: Casa Conde, Hotel Don Carlos, or similar independent properties — avoid chain hotels downtown (chain row is louder and farther from the cultural neighborhood)
  • Aug 9–11 · 2 nights

San José hotels are easy to book late-season; priority is location in the right neighborhood, not brand.

Tortuguero — Canal lodge

  • Pachira Lodge or Mawamba Lodge — both established, canal-side, include boat transfers and guided tours
  • Book a lodge that includes the La Pavona transfer and arrival canal boat — don't book transport separately
  • Most lodges are all-inclusive or half-board; confirm what meals are included
  • Aug 11–13 · 2 nights

Tortuguero has no ATM and limited card acceptance — bring cash for the village and tips.

Arenal / La Fortuna

  • Lodge with hot springs access — either on-site thermal pools or a partner arrangement with Tabacón or Baldi (the two largest hot spring complexes)
  • Arenal Observatory Lodge is on the volcano's slopes with direct views and hanging bridge access — further from town, quieter. Nayara Springs is closer to town, more resort-style, plunge pools in each villa.
  • Aug 13–15 · 2 nights

Manuel Antonio

  • Aim for a hillside property above the park with Pacific views — avoid the road-level hotels (noise, less view)
  • Makanda by the Sea (adults only, boutique, infinity pool), La Mariposa (classic clifftop views), or Hotel Si Como No (eco-certified, wildlife on grounds)
  • All three are walking distance or a short ride to the park entrance
  • Aug 15–18 · 3 nights

Flights and getting around

San Diego (SAN) → San José (SJO)

  • United via Houston (IAH) — fastest option, ~8.5 hours total. Most convenient routing.
  • American via Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW) — most frequent, ~9–10 hours total
  • Fares: typically $300–550 round-trip in August (green/low season)
  • Book as a single round-trip ticket: SAN → SJO Aug 8; SJO → SAN Aug 19
  • SJO (Juan Santamaría International) is 12 miles northwest of San José city center — 20–30 min by taxi or shuttle

Compare to Argentina: SAN → EZE is ~16 hours and $600–1,200. Costa Rica is meaningfully shorter and less expensive.

In-country transfers — no rental car needed

  • Interbus and Grayline run shared door-to-door shuttles between all four destinations. Book online in advance.
  • SJO → Tortuguero: shuttle to La Pavona dock + 1.5hr boat — total ~4 hours. Tortuguero has no road access whatsoever; the boat is the only way in.
  • Tortuguero → Arenal: boat out to La Pavona + shuttle northwest — total ~5 hours
  • Arenal → Manuel Antonio: shuttle south (possible Sarchí stop) — total ~4–5 hours. Sarchí is roughly en route; ask Interbus/Grayline if they stop or if it requires a separate arrangement.
  • Manuel Antonio → SJO: shuttle north — ~3.5 hours

Rental cars are common in Costa Rica but roads in Tortuguero don't exist and mountain roads to Arenal are steep and often unmarked — shared shuttles are genuinely the right call for this routing.

Practical

Entry — US citizens

No visa required. Passport must be valid through at least February 2027 (airlines enforce a six-month validity rule even though Costa Rica only requires validity for your stay). A return or onward ticket is required — immigration enforces this. Proof of funds ($100/month of stay) may be requested. No COVID requirements.

US State Dept. advisory as of April 2026: Level 2 — Exercise Increased Caution (petty theft, pickpocketing in tourist areas). Standard precautions apply.

Health insurance

No mandatory health insurance required at the border (unlike Argentina's 2025 rule — Costa Rica has no equivalent). However: US health insurance is not accepted by Costa Rican private hospitals. Purchase travel insurance with emergency medical before you go. A plan covering $50K+ in medical evacuation is worth the $80–120 it costs for 10 nights.

Money

Currency is the colón (CRC) — approximately 450 CRC per $1 USD as of mid-2026. US dollars are widely accepted at hotels, tour operators, national park fees, and restaurants in tourist areas. Pay in colones for local sodas, markets, and buses — paying in USD at local establishments loses value on the change rate.

Bring small USD bills in perfect condition ($5, $10, $20). Fifties and hundreds are hard to break; damaged or marked bills are refused. ATMs are reliable in San José, Quepos (Manuel Antonio), and La Fortuna — cash is useful in Tortuguero village, which has no ATM.

Weather — August

Rainy (green) season, with a consistent pattern: mornings clear, afternoon rain starting around 1–2pm, usually 1–2 hours. Plan outdoor activities before noon.

  • San José: highs ~80°F, thunderstorms possible late afternoon
  • Tortuguero: relatively drier than Pacific in August; humid, highs ~85°F
  • Arenal: heavy daily rain, especially afternoons; mornings often clear with volcano visible
  • Manuel Antonio: sunny mornings, consistent afternoon rain; highs ~85°F

Pack a lightweight rain jacket. The upside: lower prices, smaller crowds, lush green everywhere.

Phones and connectivity

US carriers have Costa Rica coverage — check your plan before leaving. A local SIM (Kolbi or Claro, ~$10–20) provides excellent data coverage in San José, Arenal, and Manuel Antonio. Tortuguero has limited connectivity; most lodges have WiFi in common areas.

Download offline maps (Google Maps or Maps.me) for all four regions before you leave the US.

Tips and customs

A 10% service charge is legally required on restaurant bills in Costa Rica — it's already in the total. Tipping beyond that is optional but appreciated for guides (~$10–20/day for naturalist guides, especially the turtle night tour). Taxi drivers: round up. Hotel staff: $1–2/day. Haggling is not a cultural norm.

Sea turtle night tour rules: No cameras, no phones, no video on the beach. No reflective gear or white clothing. Do not touch turtles or nests. Follow your guide without question — these are regulated by the Ministry of Environment.

What to pack

  • Lightweight rain jacket (leave the umbrella)
  • Hiking shoes or trail runners — boardwalks get wet
  • Water shoes for Manuel Antonio beach
  • Snorkel gear (can rent at Playa Biesanz, but your own is better)
  • DEET insect repellent — essential near canals and jungle
  • Sunscreen — reef-safe required in national parks
  • Binoculars — sloth and bird spotting is dramatically better with them
  • Dark or neutral clothing for the night turtle tour (no white, no bright colors)

Safety

Costa Rica is one of the safer countries in Central America for tourists. The US advisory is Level 2 (same level as France and Germany) due to petty theft. Standard precautions: don't leave anything visible in a car, use hotel safes, be aware in busy markets. The four destinations on this itinerary — San José arts district, Tortuguero (boat village), Arenal, Manuel Antonio — are all well-established tourist areas with good infrastructure.

Optional add-ons

  • Macuco Safari (Tortuguero or Arenal): A 4WD + forest hike + zodiac boat combo. Arenal version ends at a hidden waterfall. About 3 hours, runs mornings. Book at the lodge.
  • Brazilian side of Iguazú equivalent — Corcovado National Park (Osa Peninsula): The most biodiverse place on Earth, per National Geographic. Requires flying SJO→Puerto Jiménez or adding 3 days to the end. Too remote for this itinerary, but worth knowing it exists.
  • White-water rafting on the Pacuare River: One of the top 5 rafting rivers in the world, Class III–IV, runs through primary rainforest. Typically done as a day trip from San José or on the transfer between Tortuguero and Arenal. Interbus can often arrange it on the transfer day.
  • Night tour at Monteverde: If you want to add cloud forest without adding days, Monteverde is roughly en route from Arenal to Manuel Antonio (it's a detour, about 2 hours off the main road). A night walk through the cloud forest — frog, insects, kinkajous — is a distinct experience from anything else on this trip.
  • Isla del Caño snorkeling: The best snorkeling in Costa Rica — a protected marine reserve 12 miles offshore from Drake Bay. Requires adding 2 nights near Corcovado. Not practical for this itinerary but significantly better snorkeling than Playa Biesanz if you ever return.
  • Cahuita National Park: An alternative to Manuel Antonio on the Caribbean coast — coral reef snorkeling (the best on the Caribbean side), easier wildlife (sloths in the trees, monkeys), and a completely different Afro-Caribbean cultural vibe. Could replace Manuel Antonio if you want to stay Caribbean-side the whole trip.
